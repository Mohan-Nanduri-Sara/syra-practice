Percentages & Applications — SAT Reference

Core rules you must know cold before moving to the next topic

Key Facts

Percent of a numberpart = percent ÷ 100 × whole
Percent change(new − old) ÷ old × 100
Successive % changemultiply the factors — never add the percents
Reverse percentageoriginal = final ÷ (1 ± rate)
Percentage pointsstraight subtraction, not a percent change
Mixture (alligation-style)weight each part by its share of the total
Successive Percentages

Multiply the factors, don't add

A 10% increase followed by another 10% increase is ×1.10 × 1.10 = ×1.21 — a 21% increase overall, not 20%.

Reverse Percentage

Work backward with division

If a value is X after a p% change, the original is X ÷ (1 ± p/100). Don't just add or subtract p% from X directly.

Points vs Percent

Points are absolute, percent is relative

A score rising from 65 to 78 is a 13 percentage-point rise, but a 20% relative increase over the original 65.

Markup Then Discount

Order doesn't change the result

Multiplying by (1 + m)(1 − d) gives the same final price whether the markup or the discount is applied first.

Percent Mixture

Weight by the ratio, not a plain average

Mixing in a 1:3 ratio at 40% and 10% gives (1×40 + 3×10) ÷ 4 = 17.5% — not the simple average of 25%.

Watch Out For
Adding successive percentage changes instead of multiplying the factors
Subtracting a percent from the final value instead of dividing to reverse it
Treating percentage points and percent change as the same thing
Averaging mixture percentages evenly instead of weighting by ratio
