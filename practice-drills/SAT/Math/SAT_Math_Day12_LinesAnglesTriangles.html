Parallel Lines Cut by a Transversal

Three angle relationships to know cold

Corresponding angles are equal. Alternate interior angles are equal. Same-side (co-interior) interior angles are supplementary — this last one is the one people mistakenly treat as "equal."

Isosceles Triangles

Equal sides mean equal opposite angles

If two sides of a triangle are equal, the angles opposite those sides are equal too — that's the "base angles" rule, and it works in both directions.

Exterior Angle Theorem

A shortcut that skips a step

Instead of finding the third interior angle and subtracting from 180°, the exterior angle at a vertex equals the SUM of the two interior angles that aren't adjacent to it.

Similar Triangles

Set up the ratio with corresponding sides only

Match each side to its correct corresponding side (same relative position) before setting up the ratio — mismatching sides is the most common error.

Triangle Inequality

The two shorter sides must beat the longest

Three lengths can form a triangle only if the sum of the two shorter sides is strictly greater than the longest side.

Watch Out For
Treating same-side interior angles as equal instead of supplementary
Mismatching corresponding sides when setting up a similar-triangles ratio
Confusing an angle's exterior measure with its interior supplement
Assuming any three lengths can form a triangle without checking the inequality
