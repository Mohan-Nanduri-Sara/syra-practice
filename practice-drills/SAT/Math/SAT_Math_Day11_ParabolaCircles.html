Parabola & Circles — SAT Reference

Core rules you must know cold for the conics unit

Key Facts

Circle standard form(x−h)² + (y−k)² = r², center (h,k), radius r
Parabola vertex formy = a(x−h)² + k, vertex (h,k)
Axis of symmetryx = h (matches the vertex's x-value)
Direction of openinga > 0 opens up, a < 0 opens down
Discriminant (b²−4ac)positive=2 roots, zero=1 root, negative=0 real roots
Sum/product of rootssum = −b/a, product = c/a
Circle Equation Signs

The center's signs are OPPOSITE what's written

In (x−h)²+(y−k)²=r², a term like (x+3)² means h=−3, not +3. Always flip the sign inside the parentheses to get the center coordinate.

Radius vs r²

Take the square root before answering

The right-hand side of a circle's equation is r², not r. Forgetting to take the square root is one of the most common circle-equation errors.

Completing the Square

Works the same for circles and parabolas

Whether converting a circle's general form or a parabola's standard form, group the variable terms, complete the square, and adjust the constant to compensate.

The Leading Coefficient 'a'

Controls both direction and width

The sign of a determines whether a parabola opens up or down. The size of |a| determines width: larger |a| means a narrower, steeper curve.

Reading Graphs

Vertex and intercepts can be read directly

On a graphed parabola, the vertex is the highest/lowest point, and x-intercepts are exactly where the curve crosses the x-axis — no algebra needed if the graph is clear.

Watch Out For
Reading the center's sign directly from the equation instead of flipping it
Forgetting to take the square root of r² to get the radius
Confusing a negative discriminant (0 real roots) with a parabola that opens downward
Mixing up which curve is "narrower" when comparing two parabolas' |a| values
