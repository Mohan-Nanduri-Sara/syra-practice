Probability & Statistical Inference — SAT Reference

Core rules you must know cold for the data & probability unit

Key Facts

Basic probabilityfavorable outcomes ÷ total outcomes
Complement ruleP(not A) = 1 − P(A)
Independent events (AND)multiply the individual probabilities
Mutually exclusive (OR)add the individual probabilities
General addition ruleP(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)
Conditional probabilityP(A | B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B)
With vs. Without Replacement

The denominator shrinks without replacement

Drawing a second item without replacing the first means the total count is one smaller for the second draw — the probabilities are NOT identical.

Conditional Probability

Restrict to the given condition first

P(A | B) means: narrow your sample space down to just the cases where B happened, THEN find what fraction of those also satisfy A.

Two-Way Tables

Match the numerator to what's asked

"P(X and Y)" uses the joint cell over the grand total. "P(X | Y)" uses the joint cell over the ROW or COLUMN total for Y — not the grand total.

Expected Value

Weight each outcome by its probability

Expected value = Σ (outcome value × probability of that outcome). It's a weighted average, not just the biggest possible payout.

Sample Statistics

A sample statistic estimates the population parameter

A well-chosen sample's mean or proportion is the best available estimate for the population's mean or proportion — that's the basis of statistical inference.

Watch Out For
Using "with replacement" probabilities when the problem draws without replacement
Dividing by the grand total when the question asks for a conditional probability
Forgetting to subtract the overlap in the general addition rule
Confusing the mean, median, and range of a small dataset
