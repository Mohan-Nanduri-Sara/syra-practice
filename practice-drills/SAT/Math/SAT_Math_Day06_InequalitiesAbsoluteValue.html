The Flip Rule

Negative multiply/divide flips the sign

Whenever you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, the direction of the inequality sign reverses.

Absolute Value Equations

Split into two cases

|expression| = k means the expression equals k OR the expression equals −k. Solve both cases separately — don't just drop the absolute value bars.

Less-Than vs Greater-Than

< gives ONE interval, > gives TWO rays

|x| < k describes values BETWEEN −k and k. |x| > k describes values OUTSIDE that range — two disconnected pieces, not one interval.

Compound Inequalities

Treat it as three parts, all at once

To solve a ≤ x − b ≤ c, apply the same operation (add, subtract, multiply) to all three parts simultaneously to isolate x.

Minimum of |expression|

Absolute value can never be negative

The smallest possible value of |anything| is 0, achieved exactly when the expression inside equals 0.

Watch Out For
Forgetting to flip the inequality sign when multiplying/dividing by a negative
Only solving one case of an absolute value equation instead of both
Treating |x| > k as a single bounded interval instead of two separate rays
Applying an operation to only part of a compound inequality
