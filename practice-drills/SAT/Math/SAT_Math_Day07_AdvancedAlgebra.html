Advanced Algebra — SAT Reference

Core rules you must know cold before moving to the next topic

Key Facts

Sum of roots (Vieta's)−b ÷ a for ax² + bx + c = 0
Product of roots (Vieta's)c ÷ a for ax² + bx + c = 0
Multiplying exponentsxᵃ · xᵇ = x^(a+b) — ADD the exponents
Power of a power(xᵃ)ᵇ = x^(a×b) — MULTIPLY the exponents
Dividing exponentsxᵃ ÷ xᵇ = x^(a−b) — SUBTRACT the exponents
Discriminantb² − 4ac tells you how many real roots exist
Vieta's Formulas

Skip solving — read the roots' sum and product directly

For ax² + bx + c = 0, the sum of the roots is always −b/a and the product is always c/a — no need to factor or use the quadratic formula if you only need those.

Exponent Rules

Match the operation to the right rule

Multiplying same-base powers → ADD exponents. Raising a power to a power → MULTIPLY exponents. Dividing same-base powers → SUBTRACT exponents.

The Discriminant

b² − 4ac tells you the root situation

Positive discriminant → two real roots. Zero → exactly one repeated real root. Negative → no real roots (two complex roots).

Systems of Equations

Substitution turns two unknowns into one

Solve one equation for a variable, then substitute that expression into the other equation to reduce it to a single-variable equation.

Rational Expressions

Factor before you cancel

To simplify a rational expression, factor the numerator and denominator fully first — only cancel factors that appear in both, never individual terms.

Watch Out For
Adding exponents when the operation calls for multiplying them (or vice versa)
Confusing the sum of the roots (−b/a) with the product of the roots (c/a)
Canceling individual terms in a rational expression instead of common factors
Forgetting to check both cases when solving an equation with two solutions
