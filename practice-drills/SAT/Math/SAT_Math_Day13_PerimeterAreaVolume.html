Perimeter, Area & Volume — SAT Reference

Core rules you must know cold for the measurement unit

Key Facts

RectangleP = 2(l+w), A = lw
TriangleA = ½ × base × height
CircleC = 2πr, A = πr²
Rectangular prismV = lwh, SA = 2(lw+lh+wh)
Cylinder / ConeV = πr²h (cylinder), V = ⅓πr²h (cone)
Unit conversion scalinglength ×k, area ×k², volume ×k³
The Scaling Rule

Length, area, and volume scale differently

If every length dimension is multiplied by k, area scales by k² and volume scales by k³ — this applies to unit conversions (1 m = 100 cm, but 1 m² = 10,000 cm²) just as much as to resizing a shape.

Composite Figures

Break it into shapes you already know

Split an irregular figure into rectangles, triangles, and circular pieces, find each area separately, then add or subtract as the figure requires.

Notched Rectangles

A clean shortcut for perimeter

Cutting a rectangular notch out of a rectangle's corner doesn't change the total perimeter — the sides you add exactly replace the sides you remove. The perimeter always equals the bounding rectangle's perimeter.

Cone & Pyramid Volume

Always ⅓ of the matching prism/cylinder

A cone's volume is exactly one-third of a cylinder with the same base and height — don't forget this factor when the formula isn't given.

Working Backward

Solve for an unknown dimension by isolating it

If you're given the volume and some dimensions, plug into the formula and solve algebraically for the missing piece rather than guessing and checking.

Watch Out For
Using the linear conversion factor for an area or volume conversion instead of squaring/cubing it
Forgetting the ⅓ factor in the cone (or pyramid) volume formula
Mixing up the circumference and area formulas for a circle
Confusing perimeter with area, or volume with surface area
