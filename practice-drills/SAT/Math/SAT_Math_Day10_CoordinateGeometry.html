Coordinate Geometry — SAT Reference

Core rules you must know cold for the coordinate plane unit

Key Facts

Distance formula√[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]
Midpoint formula((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)
Slope formula(y₂−y₁) ÷ (x₂−x₁)
Reflection over x-axis(x, y) → (x, −y)
Reflection over y-axis(x, y) → (−x, y)
Section formula (ratio m:n from A)((m·x₂+n·x₁)/(m+n), (m·y₂+n·y₁)/(m+n))
Distance & Midpoint

Both come from the same two points

Distance is the length of the segment; midpoint is its center. Distance uses the Pythagorean theorem on the coordinate differences; midpoint just averages each coordinate.

Special Slopes

Vertical is undefined, horizontal is zero

A vertical line (same x-value throughout) has an undefined slope. A horizontal line (same y-value throughout) has a slope of exactly 0 — don't mix these up.

Reflections

Only one coordinate flips sign at a time

Reflecting over the x-axis flips the y-coordinate's sign. Reflecting over the y-axis flips the x-coordinate's sign. The other coordinate stays exactly the same.

Collinearity

Check that the slope is the same throughout

Three points are collinear only if the slope between the first pair equals the slope between the second pair — compute both and compare exactly.

Area From Coordinates

A right triangle's legs are just horizontal/vertical distances

When two sides of a triangle lie along the axes, its area is simply ½ × base × height, read directly from the coordinate differences.

Watch Out For
Confusing undefined slope (vertical) with zero slope (horizontal)
Flipping the wrong coordinate's sign during a reflection
Forgetting to take the square root in the distance formula
Applying the section-formula ratio in the wrong order (from the wrong endpoint)
