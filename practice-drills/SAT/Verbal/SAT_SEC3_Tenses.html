Verb Tenses — Quick Reference

Find the timeline signal in the sentence, then match the verb to it

Rule 1

Definite Past Date = Simple Past

"In 1969, astronauts WALKED..." — a specific past date always takes simple past, never present perfect.

Rule 2

"Since / For" = Present Perfect

"She HAS LIVED there since 2015" — an action starting in the past and continuing now.

Rule 3

Past Perfect for the Earlier of Two Past Events

"By the time X arrived, Y HAD ALREADY happened" — the earlier action gets "had + past participle."

Rule 4

Subjunctive "If I WERE"

Hypothetical/contrary-to-fact conditions use "were," not "was," regardless of subject.

Rule 5

Timeless Facts Stay Present — Even in Reported Speech

"The textbook explained that the Earth REVOLVES around the Sun" — general truths don't backshift.

Rule 6

Third Conditional: "Would Have" + Past Participle

"If they had practiced more, they WOULD HAVE WON" — for a hypothetical past outcome that didn't happen.

Syra's Method

1
Find the timeline signal word. "Yesterday," "since," "by the time," "next week" — these tell you exactly which tense is required.
2
Check for two past events — which happened first? The earlier one needs past perfect ("had + verb"); the later one stays simple past.
3
Watch for tense that should NOT shift. General truths and facts that are still true today stay in present tense, even inside a past-tense sentence.
Top Traps
Present perfect used with a definite past date (should be simple past)
Both past-tense verbs left as simple past when one action clearly happened before the other (needs past perfect)
"Was" used in a hypothetical "if" clause instead of subjunctive "were"
A timeless fact incorrectly backshifted to past tense inside reported speech
