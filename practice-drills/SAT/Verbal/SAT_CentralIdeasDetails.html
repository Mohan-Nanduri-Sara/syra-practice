Central Ideas & Details — Quick Reference

What the SAT tests and how to find the right answer

Type 1

Main Idea / Purpose

What is the passage primarily doing? The correct answer captures the passage as a whole — not just one detail, and not broader than what the text actually claims.

Type 2

Detail / According to

What does the text say about X? The answer must be directly stated or shown in the passage — inference is not required for these questions.

Trap 1

Too Narrow

An answer that only captures one supporting detail is wrong for a main idea question, even if it's perfectly accurate.

Trap 2

Too Broad

An answer that goes beyond what the passage actually claims is wrong — even if it sounds logical.

Trap 3

True but Not Stated

For detail questions, an answer that is probably true based on outside knowledge but not stated in the passage is always wrong.

Trap 4

Distortion

One word changed can make a true statement false. "Argues that X is impossible" vs. "argues that X is unlikely" — these are different claims.

Syra's Method

1
Read the full passage before looking at the question. For main idea questions, your job is to summarize the passage in one sentence before you read the choices. Then match.
2
For detail questions, locate the specific line. Re-read the relevant portion of the passage word for word. The correct answer will paraphrase it accurately; wrong answers distort it.
3
Apply the Goldilocks test. The correct main idea answer is not too narrow (just a detail), not too broad (beyond the text), but exactly right — the whole passage in miniature.
Top Traps
Too narrow (just a detail)
Too broad (beyond text)
True but not stated
Distortion of wording
Opposite of what text says
