Pronouns — Quick Reference

Case, agreement, and clarity — the three ways pronouns go wrong

Case

Subject vs Object

"My sister and I went" (I = subject). "The award went to my colleague and me" (me = object). Drop the other noun and test: would you say "I went" or "me went"?

Who / Whom

Subject vs Object Pronoun

Who = subject ("WHO submitted it?"). Whom = object ("TO WHOM should I address this?"). Try substituting he/him — if "him" fits, use "whom."

Agreement

Match Number to the Antecedent

"Each employee must submit THEIR timesheet" — singular antecedent, modern SAT-accepted "their." Collective nouns like "team" take "its."

Which vs That

Nonessential vs Essential Clause

"The bridge, WHICH was built in 1932, ..." (nonessential, commas). "The book THAT won the award ..." (essential, no commas).

Reference

Every Pronoun Needs ONE Clear Antecedent

If a pronoun could point to more than one noun in the sentence, it's ambiguous — the fix is usually to repeat the actual noun.

Its vs It's

Possessive vs Contraction

ITS = possessive ("the company reported ITS earnings"). IT'S = "it is" ("IT'S raining"). Never "its'" — that's not a word.

Syra's Method

1
For case questions, isolate the pronoun. Drop the other noun in a compound ("my sister and I") and read the sentence with just the pronoun — the correct case will sound right.
2
For reference questions, ask: could this pronoun point to more than one noun? If yes, the fix is almost always to replace the pronoun with the specific noun it should refer to.
3
For agreement, find the true antecedent, not just the nearest noun. "A driver should check THEIR mirrors" — antecedent is "driver," singular, but modern SAT accepts "their."
Top Traps
"Myself" or another reflexive used where a plain subject/object pronoun is needed
A pronoun that grammatically could refer to either of two nouns in the same sentence
"Who" used where the pronoun is actually the object of the verb or a preposition (needs "whom")
"It's" used as a possessive, or "its" used as a contraction — always double-check which one the sentence needs
