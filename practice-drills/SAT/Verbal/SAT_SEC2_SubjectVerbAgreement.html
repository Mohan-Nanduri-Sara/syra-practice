Subject-Verb Agreement — Quick Reference

Find the true subject first. Everything else is noise.

Rule 1

Ignore the Prepositional Phrase

"The list OF INGREDIENTS is..." — the subject is "list," not "ingredients." Cross out prepositional phrases before checking agreement.

Rule 2

AND = Plural, OR/NOR = Nearer Subject

"Maria and her brother ARE..." (plural). "Neither the coach nor the players WERE..." (agrees with the nearer subject, "players").

Rule 3

Collective Nouns Are Usually Singular

Committee, team, jury, staff — treated as one unit: "The committee IS meeting."

Rule 4

Parenthetical Phrases Don't Count

"along with," "as well as," "together with" do NOT make the subject plural: "The manager, along with her staff, IS preparing..."

Rule 5

"Number of" vs "A Number of"

"The number of applicants HAS increased" (singular). "A number of applicants HAVE applied" (plural).

Rule 6

Inverted Sentences

"There WERE several reasons..." — find the real subject after the verb and match backward.

Syra's Method

1
Find the true subject first. Cross out every prepositional phrase and parenthetical ("along with...", "as well as...") — what's left is your real subject.
2
Check if it's singular or plural. Collective nouns (team, committee) are singular. Compound subjects joined by "and" are plural.
3
For "or/nor" sentences, match the nearer subject. "Neither the coach nor the players WERE..." — look at whichever noun sits closest to the verb.
Top Traps
A plural noun sitting right before the verb, when the actual subject (earlier in the sentence) is singular
Collective nouns treated as plural when SAT convention wants singular
"along with" / "as well as" phrases mistaken for part of the subject
"One of the [plural noun] who ___" — the verb agrees with the plural noun, not "one"
