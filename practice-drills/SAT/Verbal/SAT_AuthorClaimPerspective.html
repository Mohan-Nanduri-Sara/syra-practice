Author's Claim & Perspective — Quick Reference

What the SAT tests: stance, attitude, and belief

Type 1

What is the author's claim?

What position does the author take? Not what the text mentions, but what the author argues, defends, or contends.

Type 2

Author's attitude / perspective

How does the author feel about the subject? Look for word choice, tone, framing, and what the author emphasizes or omits.

Type 3

Which statement would the author agree with?

Identify the author's position; then find the answer choice that is consistent with it. Wrong answers often capture the opposite view or a view the author explicitly critiques.

Trap 1

The view described vs. the view endorsed

An author can describe a position without endorsing it. Skeptical presentation of a view ≠ endorsement.

Trap 2

Too strong

If the author says "may suggest," the correct answer will not say "proves" or "demonstrates conclusively."

Trap 3

Confuses topic with stance

The author's topic and their stance are different things. Two authors can write about the same topic from opposite perspectives.

Syra's Method

1
Find the author's key verbs and evaluative words. "argues," "believes," "contends," "unfortunately," "remarkably" — these signal where the author stands. Neutral description uses none of these.
2
Distinguish description from endorsement. Does the author describe a view or hold it? Clues: first-person markers, direct evaluation, the structure of the argument.
3
Match the strength of the answer to the strength of the claim. If the author hedges, the answer must hedge. If the author is confident, the answer can be strong.
Top Traps
Opposite position (author critiques, not endorses)
Too strong / overclaims
Neutral description confused with endorsement
Partially correct but wrong emphasis
