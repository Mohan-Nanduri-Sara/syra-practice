Command of Evidence: Textual — Quick Reference

Two question types — quotation support and claim verification

Type 1

Quotation Support

A claim is stated, then a blank appears. Find the choice that most directly proves the specific claim — not just something related to the topic.

Type 2

Finding to Support

A student's argument is described. Which finding, if true, would most directly support it? Choose evidence that directly proves the claim.

Trap 1

Topically Related but Off-Point

A quotation that mentions the same subject is not necessarily evidence for the claim. The evidence must prove the specific assertion.

Trap 2

Too General

Evidence that proves something broader than the claim does not count as support.

Trap 3

Correct but Indirect

Evidence that makes the claim plausible but doesn't directly prove it is always a trap.

Trap 4

Contradicts Subtly

One wrong choice will look like support but actually contradicts the claim. Read every word.

Syra's Method

1
Underline the exact claim. Before looking at the choices, identify the specific assertion being made. What exactly would count as proof of this?
2
Ask: does this directly state what the claim asserts? Best evidence doesn't imply or suggest — it directly demonstrates the claim. Any answer requiring an inferential leap is wrong.
3
Compare all four. Wrong answers are often close. Only one will directly and specifically prove the claim. Eliminate topically related but off-point options first.
