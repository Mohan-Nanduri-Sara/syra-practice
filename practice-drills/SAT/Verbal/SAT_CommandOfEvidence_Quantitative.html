Command of Evidence: Quantitative — Quick Reference

Three tasks the SAT asks you to do with data

Task 1

Complete a claim

A passage makes a claim with a blank at the end. Use the data table to fill it with specific numbers that prove the point.

Task 2

Support a finding

Which row, column, or comparison in the data best supports what the researcher claims?

Task 3

Evaluate a hypothesis

The researcher proposes a pattern. Which data point is the best evidence for that pattern — and which answers just mention numbers without proving it?

Trap 1

True but irrelevant

A choice may accurately describe data that is not relevant to the specific claim. The answer must support the claim, not just be accurate.

Trap 2

Partially correct

A choice may cite real data but omit the comparison that makes the point. Complete evidence makes the comparison explicit.

Trap 3

Wrong direction

A choice may show the opposite trend to what the claim requires. Always check the direction of the comparison.

Syra's Method

1
Read the claim first, then the table. Identify exactly what the claim says — which variable, which direction, which comparison. Only then go to the table.
2
Find the specific cells that prove the claim. The correct answer will cite specific numbers. Wrong answers either omit numbers, cite wrong numbers, or make a true but irrelevant comparison.
3
State the comparison yourself. Before reading choices, say: "The data shows X > Y" or "Group A increased from X to Y." Then find the choice that says that explicitly.
Top Traps
True but irrelevant to the claim
Partially correct (missing the comparison)
Wrong direction
Accurate but about a different variable
