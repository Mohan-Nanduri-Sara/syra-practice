Parallel Structure — Quick Reference

Three places parallelism is required

Type 1

Lists

All items in a series must be the same grammatical form: all nouns, all verb phrases, all infinitives, all gerunds. Not: "She likes hiking, to swim, and ran."

Type 2

Correlative Pairs

Both...and / Either...or / Neither...nor / Not only...but also — the element after each half must be the same grammatical form. "Not only [noun] but also [noun]."

Type 3

Comparisons

Items being compared must be grammatically parallel. "She prefers hiking to [running]" — not "She prefers hiking to [when she runs]."

Test

Slot test

Replace each element with the same word and read: "She likes X, X, and X." If one slot doesn't fit the pattern, that's the broken element.

Watch for

Gerund vs. infinitive

"To run, swimming, and jump" — three different forms. Fix: "Running, swimming, and jumping" OR "To run, to swim, and to jump."

Watch for

Noun vs. clause

"She values honesty, hard work, and that people respect her" — the third element is a clause, not a noun. Fix: "honesty, hard work, and respect."

Syra's Method

1
Find the parallel structure. Look for lists (X, Y, and Z), correlative conjunctions (both X and Y), and comparisons (more X than Y). These require parallelism.
2
Identify the lead form. What grammatical form do the first two items take? The third (and fourth) must match exactly.
3
Fix the odd one out. Convert the non-parallel element to match the others. Only one answer will make all items grammatically identical.
Top Traps
"That" clause mixed with nouns
Gerund mixed with infinitive in same list
Extra words that disguise the mismatch
Correlative conjunction with mismatched halves
