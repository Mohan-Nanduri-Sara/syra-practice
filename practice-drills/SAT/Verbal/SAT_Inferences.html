Inferences — Quick Reference

The passage leaves a gap. Your job: fill it with what must follow.

The Pattern

Always the same

The passage builds an argument, observation, or line of reasoning — then stops at a blank or "suggesting that…" The answer must complete the logical movement the passage has already established.

Key Principle

Logically follows ≠ possibly true

The correct answer is not merely consistent with the passage. It is what the passage's logic demands. Wrong answers are plausible but not required by the passage.

Trap 1

Too strong

An answer that goes further than the passage supports — claiming certainty where the text only suggests — is always wrong.

Trap 2

Too narrow

An answer that only captures one detail from the passage instead of the full logical conclusion misses the point.

Trap 3

Reverses direction

One wrong answer will always follow the opposite logic. Watch for answers that contradict the passage's established direction.

Trap 4

True but not from this passage

An answer can be factually accurate or generally reasonable but not supported by this specific text. That makes it wrong.

Syra's Method

1
Identify the logical direction of the passage. Is it building toward a contrast? A conclusion? A qualification? Know where it's going before the blank.
2
Predict the completion in your own words. Before reading the choices, finish the sentence yourself. "So the passage is saying: X leads to Y, which suggests Z." Then find the choice that matches Z.
3
Test each choice: does the passage require this? Not "could this be true" or "is this consistent" — but "does the passage's logic compel this specific conclusion?" Only one answer will pass this test.
Top Traps
Too strong (overclaims)
Too narrow (misses the main point)
Reverses direction
True but not from this text
