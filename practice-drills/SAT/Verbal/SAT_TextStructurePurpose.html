Text Structure & Purpose — Quick Reference

Three tasks: main purpose, sentence function, structural analysis

Type 1

Main Purpose of Text

What is the text as a whole doing? Describing? Arguing? Illustrating? Complicating? The answer must capture what the passage does, not just what it mentions.

Type 2

Function of a Sentence

What does this specific sentence do within the text? It might introduce, support, qualify, contrast, conclude, illustrate, or complicate.

Type 3

Overall Structure

How is the text organized? Does it present two opposing views? Build to a conclusion? Introduce a claim and then support it?

Trap 1

Topic vs. Purpose

An answer that says "the text discusses X" is always wrong. The answer must say what the text does with X.

Trap 2

Too Strong or Too Weak

An answer that says "proves" when the text says "suggests," or says "criticizes" when the text "questions," misrepresents the text's stance.

Trap 3

Part for Whole

An answer that accurately describes one sentence or paragraph but not the full passage is wrong for a main purpose question.

Syra's Method

1
For main purpose: read the whole passage and ask "what is the author doing?" Describing a person? Making an argument? Complicating a common belief? Use verbs of purpose: to argue, to illustrate, to examine, to challenge, to describe.
2
For sentence function: ask what the sentence does relative to its neighbors. Does it introduce something new? Support what came before? Contrast with it? Qualify it? The function is relational — it depends on what surrounds it.
3
For structure: map the moves. Does the text present a claim then support it? Present two views then evaluate them? Introduce a problem then propose a solution? The correct answer names this architecture accurately.
Top Traps
Topic vs. purpose (what vs. why)
Too strong/weak verb choice
Part for whole
Confuses author with character described
